# How couriers work (in plain words), and what Izza copies

Written for someone who has not worked in logistics. The first part is how the industry works in general.
The second part is exactly what Izza does with it.

## The journey of a parcel

Every courier, from a post office to Uber Eats, moves a parcel through the same few "miles":

1. **First mile: collection.** Someone collects the parcel from the seller, or the seller drops it off.
2. **Hub or depot.** Parcels are gathered and sorted by area at a depot. In Izza, an **Izza point** or the
   **Izza hub** plays this part: a trusted place near the customer.
3. **Middle mile.** Long distances between cities. Izza does not need this. It is all one neighbourhood.
4. **Last mile: delivery.** A rider or driver takes it the final few streets to the customer. This is the
   hardest and most expensive part, which is why Izza uses local runners who know the streets.

## Who does what (roles)

| Role | Job | In Izza |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shipper / merchant** | Sends the parcel | **Business** |
| **Dispatcher** | Decides who does which job | The **team**, plus the job list runners pick from |
| **Driver / rider** | Collects and delivers | **Runner** |
| **Pickup point** | Holds parcels until collected | **Izza point** |
| **Recipient** | Receives it | **Customer** |

A driver only ever sees **jobs**: where to go, what to do next, who to tell. They do not see the shop's
other customers, prices, or other drivers' work. That is the rule Izza's runner screens follow.

## A job, step by step (what a driver app does)

1. **Offer / accept.** A job appears on a list. The driver taps **Accept** (or the dispatcher assigns it).
   Accepting is a promise. If the driver cannot do it, they **give it back** so someone else can.
2. **Go to pickup.** Navigation to the address. Courier apps show the address, a contact number and any note.
3. **Proof of pickup.** The driver confirms they have the parcel (a scan or a tap, sometimes a photo).
   From this moment the parcel is "in transit" and the seller is told.
4. **Status updates.** At each step the app records an **event** with a time: "on the way", "arrived",
   "collected", "delivered". Customers see these in tracking. Tracking pages are just this list of events.
5. **Drop at the hub / point.** Same again: arrive, hand over, the point confirms.
6. **Out for delivery.** The driver takes it from the hub to the door.
7. **Proof of delivery (POD).** The driver proves the right person got it: a **PIN or code** the customer
   reads out, a photo, a signature, or all of them. Izza uses the customer's **4-digit delivery code**.
8. **If it fails.** Nobody home, address not found, customer refused. The driver records a **reason**
   (couriers insist on a reason so disputes can be settled) and the parcel is **tried again, returned to
   the hub, or sent back to the seller**. Izza's rule: it goes **back to the Izza point** and the customer is
   told to collect it there.

## Words you will hear

- **Dispatch**: handing a job to a driver.
- **ETA**: estimated time of arrival.
- **POD**: proof of delivery.
- **Exception**: anything that goes wrong (not home, damaged, refused).
- **Attempt**: one try at delivering. Couriers often allow two or three.
- **Reverse logistics / returns**: sending things back.
- **COD**: cash on delivery. Izza does not do this yet.

## What Izza does with all this

- **Runners work from jobs, not from the customer app.** Their home is a job list. No "Send" tab.
- **Accept, then work the steps.** Each step is one big button, and each one tells the customer.
- **Give the job back** if you cannot do it.
- **Quick updates** ("on my way", "I have arrived") and a **WhatsApp message to the customer** ready to send.
- **Report a problem** with a reason, so there is a record.
- **Delivery needs the customer's 4-digit code**, so a parcel cannot be marked delivered by mistake.
- **A failed delivery goes back to the Izza point.**
- **Everyone sees only what their job needs.** A runner sees the customer's number only for the job they accepted.
